# Scoring

The goal is an honest estimate from a short test. A level set too high breaks the plan in week two.

## Conversion table

| CEFR | IELTS (estimate) | TOEFL iBT (estimate) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | below 3.0 | 0-8 | basic words and phrases |
| A2 | 3.0-3.5 | 9-18 | simple, routine exchanges |
| B1 | 4.0-5.0 | 19-46 | independent on familiar topics |
| B2 | 5.5-6.5 | 47-86 | comfortable, including some abstract topics |
| C1 | 7.0-8.0 | 87-109 | fluent, flexible, precise |
| C2 | 8.5-9.0 | 110-120 | near-native |

These are common approximate links, not official equivalences. Give TOEFL as a range.

The B1/B2 border: IELTS 5.0 is the top of B1 and 5.5 is the bottom of B2. "B1 with some B2" is B1 and IELTS 5.0. Give B2 only when the learner's own grammar and production are consistently at B2, not only their reading.

## Sub-skill descriptors

Pick the level that matches the learner's usual performance, not the best or worst moment.

**Grammar.** A2: present and basic past; frequent article, preposition and agreement errors; very simple sentences. B1: present, past, future and present perfect; first conditional; errors in perfect tenses, conditionals and complex clauses. B2: most tenses and conditionals with occasional slips; passive, reported speech, relative clauses; errors do not block meaning. C1: inversion, cleft sentences, mixed conditionals, fine modality; rare minor errors.

**Vocabulary.** A2: concrete, frequent words for daily topics. B1: familiar topics, some phrasal verbs and collocations, gaps on abstract topics. B2: good range with some less common words, mostly correct collocations, some register awareness. C1: precise and idiomatic, controls connotation and register, paraphrases smoothly.

**Reading.** A2: literal facts from short simple texts. B1: main ideas and most details of everyday texts. B2: argument and detail in longer texts, most inference. C1: nuance, tone and implied meaning in dense texts.

**Listening.** A2: slow, clear speech on familiar topics, with replays. B1: gist and key details at near-natural speed. B2: natural-speed speech on familiar topics, most detail and some implication. C1: fast, implicit, abstract speech.

**Writing and speaking (production).** A2: short separate simple sentences. B1: sustains familiar topics, links with *and, but, because, so*. B2: argues a position with reasons, uses *although, whereas, however*, varied structure. C1: fluent and well organised, uses subordination, hedging and signposting.

## Overall level

Start from the middle of the sub-skill levels. Grammar and production weigh most, because receptive skills usually look higher than what the learner can produce. Report the spread: "B1 speaking, B2 reading" shapes the plan directly. When the evidence is thin, give a range ("B1, possibly B1+").

## Discipline

- Back every level with evidence: a quoted error or a quoted strong sentence.
- Typos and one-off slips do not lower a level. Repeated patterns do.
- A learner who tries C1 structures and gets half of them right is usually a solid B2; name the reach as a strength.
- Name the one weakness that, once fixed, helps most. The plan starts with it.

## Result message

Write it in plain words at the learner's level, not in examiner terms. Template (replace every value):

```
This is an estimate from a short test, not an official score. It is provisional: your first two weeks of lessons will confirm it.

Your level: B1 (IELTS estimate 5.0, TOEFL estimate 38-46)

- Grammar: B1. Past and present are solid; conditionals still slip ("If it will rain" -> "If it rains").
- Vocabulary: B1+. Good on everyday topics; you searched for words on the abstract question.
- Reading: B2. You followed the argument and the hidden meaning.
- Listening: B1. You got the main idea and the details of the B1 clip; the B2 clip needed three plays.
- Writing: B1. Clear and connected, mostly short sentences.
- Speaking: B1. You kept talking; articles dropped often ("<quoted error from the learner's answer>").

Your strength: you understand more than you produce, so we have a lot to build on.
Your main focus: articles and longer sentences when you speak.
```
