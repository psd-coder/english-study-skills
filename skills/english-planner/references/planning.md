# Planning

## Feasibility

Plan with about 100 hours of focused study per IELTS half band, and about 130 above IELTS 6.5. For the current and target level, use the learner's IELTS estimate, or these working values: A2 3.5, B1 5.0, B2 6.0, C1 7.0. So B1 to B2 is 2 half bands, about 200 hours, which matches the common estimate for one CEFR level. These numbers only power an honest verdict; do not present them as precise.

When the goal is functional ("confident in work calls", "write clear emails"), translate it into a working level for the math only, tell the learner you did so, and plan and report in the functional terms. Rough mapping: travel and small talk, B1+; work meetings and emails, B2; following native media easily, B2 to C1.

1. Gap = target level minus current level, in half bands.
2. Hours needed = gap times about 100 (130 for half bands above 6.5).
3. Hours available = minutes per day times days per week times weeks until the deadline, divided by 60. With no deadline, use 26 weeks.
4. Verdict by available divided by needed: 1.3 or more, comfortable. 1.0 to 1.3, realistic. 0.6 to 1.0, a stretch. Below 0.6, not realistic by the deadline.

When the verdict is "not realistic", narrow the goal by area, not by level: "not full B2 by March, but confident in your weekly calls, if we aim the time there". Then name the real choice: more minutes a day, or more months.

## The four strands

Each strand is about a quarter of the time by default (Nation's four strands):

- **Input** (meaning-focused input): listening and reading for meaning. Skill: `english-practice` listening or reading.
- **Output** (meaning-focused output): writing and speaking to say something real. Skill: `english-practice` writing or speaking.
- **Language** (language-focused learning): grammar and chunks studied directly. Skill: `english-grammar`, `english-vocab`.
- **Fluency**: easy material used fast, to make known language automatic. Skill: `english-practice` with `fluency` (timed retell, speed reading of easy text, 4-3-2 speaking).

Every session also starts with a review warm-up of due cards, whatever its slot. That is how spacing reaches every day.

## Slot allocation

One study day is one slot. Give each strand `weight * study_days / 100` slots, round down, and give the remaining slots to the strands with the largest remainders. On a tie, prefer the strand of the learner's focus, then the strand that got fewer slots last week. A strand with no slot this week gets one first next week, so every strand appears at least every two weeks.

Then place the slots:

- Put a grammar lesson early in the week, so its homework is graded and reviewed later in the week.
- Put the output slot after the language slot it can use.
- Alternate listening and reading across weeks inside the input strand, and writing and speaking inside output, unless the goal asks for one of them (a speaking goal: speaking twice as often as writing).
- With 15 minutes a day, a slot is one activity. With 45 minutes or more, add a short fluency task to the language and input slots.

## plan.md format

```
# Study plan: <name>

Goal: <functional goal> (working level <B2> for the timeline)
Verdict: <realistic>. <one line why>
Start: <date>. Checkpoints every 6 weeks.

## Roadmap
- Block 1 (weeks 1-6): <focus>. By the end you can <observable outcome>.
- Block 2 (weeks 7-12): <focus>. By the end you can <...>.
- Later: <next horizon>

## Weights
input 25, output 30, language 25, fluency 20 (changed in week 3: more output, speaking errors up)

## This week (week 3, from <Monday date>)
Themes: grammar <articles with known nouns>; chunks <meetings: agreeing and disagreeing>; listening level <B1+>.
- mon: language, grammar lesson (articles 2)
- tue: input, listening (BBC 6 Minute English, B1-B2)
- wed: language, chunks session (meetings)
- thu: output, speaking (retell of Tuesday's episode)
- fri: fluency, 4-3-2 speaking on the week's topic
End of week target: <one observable thing>
```

Keep earlier weeks out of `plan.md`; the sessions log is the history.
