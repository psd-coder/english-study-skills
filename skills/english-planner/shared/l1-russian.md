# Russian speakers: typical traps

Use this file when `l1` is Russian (it also fits Ukrainian and Belarusian speakers well). Name the trap before the learner falls into it, and check the learner's own error samples first: their real data outranks this list.

For other first languages, infer the traps from how that language differs from English in articles, tense and aspect, word order, prepositions and plural marking, and confirm them against the learner's samples.

## Articles (the biggest one)

Russian has no articles, so learners drop them, or add `the` everywhere after they learn the rule.

- Missing `a` with singular countable nouns: "He is engineer", "I have meeting".
- `the` for something both people know: "Close door, please", "I talked to manager".
- `the` with generalisations: "The life is hard", "I like the music" (in general).
- Names and places: "the London", "in the Germany", but "the Netherlands", "the USA", "the UK".
- Ordinals and superlatives need `the`: "2nd idea" -> "the 2nd idea".
- Teach articles as questions: Is it countable? Singular? Does the listener know which one? Is this a general statement?

## Tense and aspect

Russian has one past tense and expresses completion with verb aspect (делать/сделать), so the English perfect has no direct match.

- Past simple where English needs the present perfect: "I already did it", "Did you ever been...".
- Present simple or continuous for a state that started in the past: "I live here for five years", "I am working here since 2020" -> "have lived", "have been working".
- `will` in time and condition clauses: "When I will finish, I will call you", "If it will rain".
- Continuous with stative verbs: "I am knowing", "I am understanding".
- No sequence of tenses: "He said he is tired" -> "was tired".

## Word order and structure

- Questions without do-support: "Where you work?", "Why you didn't tell me?".
- Embedded questions with question order: "Tell me where is the office" -> "where the office is". "What L1 cue is?" -> "what an L1 cue is".
- Missing dummy subject: "Is cold today", "Is important to test" -> "It is".
- Missing `there is`: "In my city is a big park" -> "There is a big park in my city".
- Adverb placed between verb and object: "I like very much coffee" -> "I like coffee very much".
- "The more advanced level they have" -> "The more advanced their level is".

## Agreement and countability

- `has` and `have` after third person singular, including after long subjects: "The team have" (US: has), "Each of the tests have" -> "has".
- Uncountable nouns used as countable: informations, advices, news are, feedbacks, softwares, equipments, furnitures.
- `less` with countable plurals: "less errors" -> "fewer errors".
- `people` is plural: "people is" -> "people are".

## Prepositions (chosen from the Russian equivalent)

- depend from -> depend on (зависеть от)
- married on -> married to (женат на)
- arrive to Berlin -> arrive in Berlin, arrive at the office (приехать в)
- in the internet -> on the internet (в интернете)
- on the photo -> in the photo (на фото)
- angry on -> angry with / at (злиться на)
- consist from -> consist of (состоять из)
- explain me -> explain to me; listen music -> listen to music
- discuss about -> discuss (обсуждать что-то)
- in the weekend -> at / on the weekend; at summer -> in summer

## Collocations and verbs

- `do` and `make`: make a mistake, make a decision, make a call, do a review, do a task.
- `say` and `tell`: tell someone, say something (to someone). "He said me" -> "He told me".
- Reflexive transfer: "I feel myself good" (чувствую себя) -> "I feel good".
- "I want that you check" (хочу, чтобы) -> "I want you to check".
- Adjective where English needs an adverb: "It works correct" -> "correctly".
- Double negation: "I don't know nothing" -> "anything".
- "Let's discuss about it" and "return back" (вернуться назад) carry an extra word.

## Punctuation and capitalization

- Comma splices from Russian comma rules: "I checked it, it works" -> a full stop, a semicolon or "and".
- No comma before `that` in English: "He said, that" -> "He said that".
- Capital letters for languages, nationalities, days and months: English, Russian, Monday, October.
- The pronoun `I` is always capital.

## Pronunciation (for speaking and shadowing)

- `th` as /s/, /z/, /f/ or /t/: think, this, three.
- `w` and `v` mixed: west/vest, wine/vine.
- Short and long vowels merged: ship/sheep, full/fool, live/leave.
- Final consonants devoiced: bed sounds like "bet", dog like "dock".
- `h` said as the harsh Russian х.
- Word stress moved: say deVElop (not DEvelop), COMfortable (not comFORtable).
- Flat intonation that sounds rude in requests; practise rising tone for polite questions.
